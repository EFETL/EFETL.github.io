ARTICLE
The Doctor Who Listens

A study in Britain timed how long patients spoke before a physician interrupted them. The average was eleven seconds.

That habit costs time rather than saving it. When patients are allowed to finish, they often mention the one symptom that explains everything, and fewer tests are ordered later.

One clinic in Leeds now trains its doctors to stay silent for the first ninety seconds. Complaints have fallen by half, and the number of patients sent back to a specialist a second time has dropped sharply. The doctors report one more benefit: their working day feels calmer.

全文中譯

英國一項研究計算了病人在被醫師打斷前能說多久。平均只有十一秒。

這個習慣不但沒省下時間，反而更花時間。當病人被允許把話說完，他們往往會提到那個足以解釋一切的關鍵症狀，之後也就不必開那麼多檢查。

利茲的一家診所現在訓練醫師在最初的九十秒保持沉默。投訴減少了一半，被二度轉回專科醫師的病人數量也大幅下降。醫師們還提到另一個好處：他們的工作日感覺更平靜了。
