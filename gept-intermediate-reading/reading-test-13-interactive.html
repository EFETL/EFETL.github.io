ARTICLE
Six Evenings Beat One Long Night

Students who study a subject on six short evenings remember far more than those who cram the night before. In one test, two groups of university juniors learned the same fifty words. A month later, the spaced group recalled 68 percent; the crammers recalled only 29 percent.

Cramming is not useless. It can rescue a student on the morning of an exam. The problem is that the words vanish almost as fast as they arrive.

The researchers advise a simple rule: review new material within a day, then again after a week. Ten quiet minutes, they say, beat two hours of panic.

全文中譯

把一個科目分成六個短短的晚上來讀的學生，記住的內容遠多於考前一晚才死記硬背的人。在一項測驗中，兩組大學三年級學生背同樣的五十個單字。一個月後，分散學習的那組記得百分之六十八，臨時抱佛腳的那組只記得百分之二十九。

死記硬背並非毫無用處，它能在考試當天早上救學生一命。問題在於，這些字消失的速度幾乎和進來時一樣快。

研究人員建議一個簡單的原則：新教材要在一天內複習一次，一週後再複習一次。他們說，安靜的十分鐘勝過兩小時的慌亂。
