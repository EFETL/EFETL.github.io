Booking a Portrait Session

Dear Ms. Kuo,

My name is Andrew Peng. My grandmother will turn ninety on October 12, and I would like to book a family portrait session at Cedarwood Studio that weekend. There will be fourteen of us, including three small children.

Your website lists a one-hour indoor session for NT$4,500, while the outdoor package costs NT$6,000. Since my grandmother tires easily, we prefer the indoor studio. Could you tell me whether Saturday afternoon is still open, and how many edited photos are included? We would also like one large print for her living room.

Thank you,
Andrew Peng

全文中譯

郭小姐您好：

我叫 Andrew Peng。我的祖母十月十二日將滿九十歲，我想在那個週末於 Cedarwood 攝影工作室預約一場家庭合照。我們共有十四人，其中包含三名幼童。

貴公司網站上寫著一小時的室內拍攝為新台幣 4,500 元，戶外方案則是 6,000 元。由於祖母容易疲累，我們比較希望在室內棚拍。可否告訴我週六下午是否還有空檔，以及方案內含幾張修圖後的照片？我們另外想沖洗一張大尺寸照片，掛在她的客廳。

謝謝您，
Andrew Peng 敬上

ARTICLE
Why We Keep Taking the Same Photo

Stand at any famous viewpoint and you will see a line of visitors holding up their phones at exactly the same angle. Researchers at a Dutch university call this the copy effect: once an image becomes popular online, travelers feel they must bring home the very same shot.

The habit is not harmless. In several small villages, crowds gathering for a single view have worn down narrow paths and annoyed residents. A few towns now put up signs asking visitors to spread out.

Yet the same researchers found a cure. Tourists who were asked to photograph something ordinary — a doorway, a market basket — reported enjoying the trip more, and their pictures were far more varied.

全文中譯

站在任何一個知名觀景點，你都會看到一排遊客以完全相同的角度舉起手機。荷蘭一所大學的研究人員把這稱為「複製效應」：一旦某張影像在網路上爆紅，旅人就覺得自己非帶回一模一樣的畫面不可。

這個習慣並非無害。在好幾個小村莊，為了同一個景色而聚集的人潮已經踩壞了狹窄的小徑，也惹惱了居民。現在有些小鎮豎起告示牌，請遊客分散開來。

不過同一批研究人員也找到了解方。被要求去拍攝平凡事物的遊客——一道門口、一只市場提籃——表示自己更享受這趟旅程，而且他們拍出的照片也豐富多樣得多。

解析全文從批評盲目複製到提出「拍自己的畫面更享受旅程」，故主旨為尋找自己的角度才能真正看見一個地方。其餘選項皆過於極端或文中未提。
