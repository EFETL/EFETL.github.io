Passage 1
Putting a Name to a Feeling

Psychologists say that people who can name their feelings handle them far better. A child who says only that the day was bad may in fact feel 11 before a test, or deep 12 after losing a match. Teachers therefore ask students to keep a short evening diary. Writing one honest line each night can 13 a worried mind more than an hour of advice. Over time young writers are often 14 by how many different feelings appear on a single page. This habit solves no problems by itself, yet most students end the term calmer and far more 15 with the person they are becoming.

全文中譯心理學家說，能替自己的感受命名的人，處理起情緒來好得多。一個只說「今天很糟」的孩子，其實可能是考試前感到焦慮，或是輸掉比賽後深深失望。因此老師會請學生寫簡短的晚間日記。每晚誠實寫下一句話，安撫一顆煩憂的心，效果勝過一小時的勸告。久而久之，這些小作者常會驚訝地發現，同一頁上竟出現這麼多不同的感受。這個習慣本身不會消除任何問題，但多數學生在學期末都變得更平靜，也更能接受自己正在成為的樣子。

The Voice on the Help Line

Every Tuesday evening Mei-ling works as a 16 on a telephone help line. Callers are strangers, yet within minutes they tell her things they hide from every close 17 in their lives. Some are simply lonely; others still 18 for a parent who died last spring. Mei-ling rarely gives advice, because she has learned that a caller in the dark needs a steady voice far more than a 19 plan. When the line finally goes quiet, she often hears a small laugh instead of tears, and for her that sound is a real 20 at the end of a tiring day.

全文中譯每個星期二晚上，美玲在一條電話輔導專線擔任志工。來電者都是陌生人，但不到幾分鐘，他們就會說出連身邊每個熟識的人都瞞著的事。有些人只是寂寞；有些人仍在為去年春天過世的父母哀傷。美玲很少給建議，因為她學到，身處黑暗中的來電者需要的是一個穩定的聲音，遠勝過一份有建設性的計畫。當線路終於安靜下來，她常聽見的是一聲輕笑而非哭泣；對她而言，那個聲音是疲累一天結束時真正的幸事。
