Dear Editor,

I have read the Harborview Daily for eleven years, so I was sorry to find a mistake in Tuesday's front-page story about the new bus line. The article said the city would spend 500 million dollars, but the figure on the city website is 50 million. One extra zero can completely change how readers judge the plan.

I do not believe your reporter meant to mislead anyone. Still, I hope the paper will print a clear correction on the front page, not hidden among the advertisements. Readers trust you, and that trust is worth protecting.

Yours truly,
Wendy Kao

ARTICLE
When Photos Lie

A photograph used to be treated as proof. Today, cheap software can move a building, add a crowd, or place a famous face on a stranger's body. Because the result looks smooth and natural, our eyes are easily fooled.

Experts say the answer is not to doubt every image, but to ask a few simple questions. Where was the picture first published? Do the shadows fall in the same direction? Does any other source show the same scene? Machines that detect changed images are improving, yet they still make mistakes. In the end, careful human habits remain our best defense against pictures that lie.
