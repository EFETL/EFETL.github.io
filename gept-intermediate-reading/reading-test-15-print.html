ARTICLE
When Coffee Houses Were Newsrooms

Three hundred years ago, the coffee houses of London were far more than places to drink. For the price of one cup, a visitor could sit all day, read the newspapers on the wall, and listen to merchants argue about ships and prices. People called them penny universities, because so much could be learned there for a single penny.

Women were usually not welcome, and some doctors warned that the new drink was bad for health. Neither complaint stopped the trend. By 1700 London had hundreds of coffee houses, and several of today's banks and newspapers began as small businesses inside them.
