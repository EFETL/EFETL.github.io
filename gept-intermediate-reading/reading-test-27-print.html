ARTICLE
Growing Up with Two Languages

Parents once worried that raising a child with two languages would cause confusion. Research now tells a different story. Children who hear two languages every day may start speaking a little later, but they usually catch up by the age of four.

These children also seem better at switching between tasks, perhaps because their brains practice choosing the right word all day long. The key, researchers say, is regular use: a language heard only at holidays is quickly forgotten. Many families follow a simple rule — one parent, one language — so that both tongues stay alive at home.
