ARTICLE
The Quiet Return of the Camp Stove

For many campers a fire is the heart of the night. Yet in dry seasons, when a single spark can start a wildfire, more and more parks forbid open flames, and visitors are turning to small gas stoves instead.

The change is not only about safety. A stove boils water in three minutes, leaves no ash, and works in wind or light rain. Wood, on the other hand, must be bought or gathered, and its smoke can bother neighbors at a crowded site.

Still, few campers give up fire completely. When the risk is low, they light one simply to sit around it and talk.

全文中譯

對許多露營者而言，火是夜晚的核心。然而在乾季，一顆火星就可能引發野火，因此越來越多公園禁止明火，遊客轉而改用小型瓦斯爐。

這項改變不只關乎安全。爐子三分鐘就能把水煮開，不留灰燼，而且在風中或小雨中都能使用。相對地，木柴必須購買或撿拾，而它的煙在擁擠的營地會打擾鄰居。

儘管如此，很少有露營者完全放棄生火。當風險不高時，他們仍會生一堆火，單純為了圍著它坐下來聊天。
