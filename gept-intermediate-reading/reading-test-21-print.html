ARTICLE
The Two-Minute Rule for Bad Days

When people feel low, advice often arrives in large sizes: change your job, move house, exercise for an hour a day. Therapists at a Taipei clinic argue that big plans usually fail on bad days, because a heavy mood removes the very energy needed to begin them.

Their answer is the two-minute rule. Choose an action so small that refusing it feels silly: open the curtains, wash one cup, step outside the front door. The point is not the cup. Movement of any kind tells the brain that the day is not over.

Patients who tried the rule reported fewer whole days lost to low mood, though the clinic warns that it is a first step, not a treatment.
