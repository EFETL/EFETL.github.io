ARTICLE
Why Averages Can Fool You

A town reports that the average income of its households is NT$95,000 a month. That sounds comfortable, yet half the families there earn under NT$48,000.

The reason is simple. An average is pulled upward by a few very large numbers. Ten ordinary households and one millionaire can produce an average that describes nobody in the street.

Careful reporters therefore give a second figure, the middle value, which splits the group into two equal halves. When the two numbers sit far apart, something unusual is hiding in the data, and a chart with a single bar will never show it.

全文中譯

某個城鎮公布，當地家庭的平均月收入是新台幣 95,000 元。這聽起來相當寬裕，然而那裡有一半的家庭月收入不到 48,000 元。

原因很簡單。平均值會被少數幾個極大的數字往上拉。十戶普通人家加上一位百萬富翁，就能算出一個誰也不符合的平均數。

因此謹慎的記者會再給一個數字，也就是把群體分成相等兩半的中位數。當這兩個數字相差很遠時，資料裡就藏著不尋常的東西，而只有一根長條的圖表永遠顯示不出來。
