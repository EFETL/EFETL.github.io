The Bike a Driver Never Sees

Traffic researchers call it the looked-but-failed-to-see error. A driver stops at a corner, turns his head, and still pulls out right in front of a bicycle. His eyes did their job, yet his brain was hunting for the wide shape of a car and simply threw the thin bike away.

Two things help. First, a rider who moves a little toward the middle of the lane is much harder to ignore than one hugging the curb. Second, a moving light on the front wheel catches attention better than a steady lamp, because the human eye is built to notice movement.

A Letter to the Road Office

Dear Sir or Madam,

I ride to work along Fuxing Street every morning, and I would like to report a danger at the corner of Fuxing Street and Ninth Avenue. A large banyan tree now hides the stop sign, so drivers turning right hardly slow down at all. Last Tuesday a student on a bicycle was almost hit there.

Could your department cut the branches and repaint the crossing lines, which have nearly disappeared? A mirror on the wall opposite would help too. I am willing to meet an officer at the corner at any time.

Yours faithfully,
Tsai Yu-han
