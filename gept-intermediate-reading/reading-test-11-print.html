ARTICLE
Short Practice, Big Progress

Music teachers used to tell students to practice for at least an hour every day. Recent studies, however, suggest that shorter sessions may work better. In one experiment, students who practiced twenty minutes twice a day learned a new piece faster than those who practiced for a full hour at once.

Researchers explain that the brain needs rest in order to store new movements. During a break, it quietly repeats what the fingers have just done. Sleep matters too: pianists who practiced in the evening and slept well played more smoothly the next morning. The message is simple — practice often, and give your brain time to catch up.
