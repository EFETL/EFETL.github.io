ARTICLE
The Seconds Before the Shaking

When an earthquake begins, two kinds of waves travel through the ground. The first is fast but gentle; the second is slower and does the real damage. Sensors buried across the island feel the first wave and send a message before the second one arrives.

That message may reach your phone only ten or twenty seconds ahead of the shaking. It sounds like very little, yet those seconds are enough to stop a high-speed train, close a gas valve, or move a child under a desk.

Experts add that an alert is useless if people do not know what to do, so schools practice the drill twice a year.

全文中譯

地震開始時，有兩種波在地底傳遞。第一種速度快但較溫和；第二種較慢，卻是真正造成破壞的元凶。埋設在全島各地的感測器會感應到第一種波，並在第二種波抵達之前發出訊息。

這則訊息傳到你手機時，可能只比搖晃早了十到二十秒。這聽起來微不足道，但這幾秒已足以讓高鐵停車、關上瓦斯開關，或把孩子移到桌子底下。

專家補充說，如果民眾不知道該怎麼做，警報就毫無用處，因此學校每年演練兩次。
