ARTICLE
The Clock That Never Hurries

An atomic clock in a locked room in Colorado loses less than one second in a hundred million years. Few people need that kind of accuracy, yet almost everyone uses it, because every phone map depends on satellites that carry such clocks.

The scientific idea behind them is simple. Atoms of one metal always take exactly the same tiny time to change their state, so counting those changes is safer than counting the swings of any pendulum.

Engineers are now building a clock ten times better. Their aim is not to be on time for dinner, but to measure how gravity slightly slows time itself.

全文中譯

科羅拉多州一間上鎖房間裡的原子鐘，一億年才會差不到一秒。很少人需要這種精準度，卻幾乎人人都在使用它，因為每一個手機地圖都仰賴搭載這種時鐘的衛星。

它背後的科學原理很簡單。同一種金屬的原子改變狀態所需的極短時間永遠完全相同，因此計算這些變化，比計算任何鐘擺的擺動都可靠。

工程師目前正在打造一個精準度再高十倍的時鐘。他們的目標不是為了準時吃晚餐，而是要測量重力如何讓時間本身稍微變慢。
