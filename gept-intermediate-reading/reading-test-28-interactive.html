Whose Name Do the Children Take?

For centuries in Taiwan, a newborn baby automatically received the father's family name. A change in the law in 2007 allowed parents to decide the matter together, and either name is now perfectly legal.

The change has been slow to show. Official figures suggest that fewer than one baby in twenty carries the mother's name. Some couples still fear that older relatives will feel hurt; others simply follow habit. Yet young parents in cities increasingly discuss the question before the birth, and a few families give the first child one name and the second child the other.

全文中譯

數百年來，台灣的新生兒自動冠上父親的姓氏。二〇〇七年的法律修改讓父母可以共同決定，如今從父姓或從母姓都完全合法。

這項改變的效果來得很慢。官方數字顯示，跟隨母姓的嬰兒不到二十分之一。有些夫妻仍擔心長輩心裡會不舒服，有些人則只是照著習慣走。不過，城市裡的年輕父母越來越常在孩子出生前就討論這個問題，也有少數家庭讓第一個孩子從父姓、第二個孩子從母姓。
