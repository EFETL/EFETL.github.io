A Letter from Aunt Mei

Dear Hsiao-en,

Your mother tells me you are writing a report on our family, so here is what I remember.

Your great-grandmother came south alone at seventeen and worked as a maid for a family in Tainan. She married at twenty-four and had six children; two of them died as infants. The old photograph on your grandmother's wall was taken the week the youngest was born.

She never learned to read, yet she insisted that every daughter finish school. Your grandmother became the first nurse in the village because of her.

Come on Sunday and bring a notebook. I will find the box of letters in the attic before you arrive.

Love,
Aunt Mei

全文中譯

小恩：

你媽媽說你在寫一份關於我們家族的報告，所以我把記得的事寫下來給你。

你的曾祖母十七歲時獨自南下，在台南一戶人家幫傭當女僕。她二十四歲結婚，生了六個孩子，其中兩個在嬰兒時期夭折。你奶奶牆上那張老照片，是最小的孩子出生那一週拍的。

她從沒學過認字，卻堅持每個女兒都要念完書。你奶奶之所以成為村裡第一位護理師，就是因為她。

星期天過來吧，記得帶筆記本。我會在你到之前把閣樓裡那箱信找出來。

愛你的
阿美姑姑

ARTICLE
Waiting at the School Gate

In many towns the person waiting at the school gate is not a parent but a grandparent. As more couples take jobs in distant cities, children are left in the care of the older generation. Officials in one county say that almost one child in five now lives this way.

The arrangement has clear benefits. Grandparents pass on dialect, cooking and family stories that a busy household often loses.

Yet the burden is heavy. Many carers are over seventy and manage the housework alone. Support groups now offer them free health checks, and one afternoon a week when somebody else looks after the children.

全文中譯

在許多鄉鎮，在校門口等待的人不是父母，而是祖父母。隨著愈來愈多夫妻到遠方的城市工作，孩子便交由上一代照顧。某縣的官員表示，現在幾乎每五個孩子就有一個是這樣長大的。

這種安排有明顯的優點。祖父母會把方言、家常菜和家族故事傳下去，而這些常在忙碌的家庭中流失。

然而負擔很重。許多照顧者已年逾七十，還得獨自打理家務。互助團體現在提供他們免費健康檢查，以及每週一個下午由別人代為照顧孩子的時間。
