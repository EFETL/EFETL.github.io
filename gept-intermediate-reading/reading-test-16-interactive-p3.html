Buying a Used Bicycle

Hi Marco,

I saw your post about the blue road bike and I am still interested. Before we meet, I have a few questions. How old is the bike, and have the chain or the brakes ever been replaced? The photos show a small crack near the seat — is it only in the paint?

I can come to Banqiao this Saturday afternoon. If the bike rides well, I will pay in cash on the spot. Would you accept NT$4,500? Your post says NT$5,200, but the tires look worn and I will have to buy new ones.

Please bring the repair records if you still have them.

Thanks,
Denise

全文中譯

馬可你好：

我看到你關於那台藍色公路車的貼文，我仍然很有興趣。在見面之前，我有幾個問題。這台車幾年了？鏈條或煞車有換過嗎？照片上座墊附近有一道小裂痕——那只是漆面上的嗎？

我這週六下午可以到板橋。如果騎起來順暢，我會當場付現。你能接受 NT$4,500 嗎？你的貼文寫 NT$5,200，但輪胎看起來很磨損，我還得買新的。

如果你還留著維修紀錄，請一併帶來。

謝謝，
丹妮絲

ARTICLE
The Rise of Repair Cafés

On the first Saturday of every month, a community hall in Taichung fills with broken toasters, torn coats and silent radios. This is a repair café, where volunteers with tools help visitors mend their own belongings for free. The idea began in Amsterdam in 2009 and has since spread to more than two thousand towns.

Organizers say the goal is not only to save objects from the rubbish. Visitors learn simple skills, meet their neighbors, and start to see repair as normal rather than strange. About seventy percent of the items brought in leave the hall working again.

全文中譯

每個月的第一個星期六，台中的一處社區活動中心裡擺滿了壞掉的烤麵包機、破損的外套和發不出聲音的收音機。這裡是「修理咖啡館」，帶著工具的志工在此免費協助來訪者修好自己的物品。這個構想二○○九年始於阿姆斯特丹，此後已擴散到超過兩千個城鎮。

主辦者表示，目標不只是把東西從垃圾堆中救回來。來訪者學會簡單的技能、認識鄰居，並開始把修理視為理所當然而非奇怪的事。送來的物品中，約有七成離開會場時已能再次運作。
