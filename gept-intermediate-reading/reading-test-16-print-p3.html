The Circus Without Animals

Twenty years ago a circus in Canada made a strange decision: no lions, no elephants, no horses. Some people predicted that it would close within a season.

Instead it grew. Its performers trained for years as dancers and gymnasts, and its shows told one story from beginning to end, more like an opera than a fair.

Today many old circuses have followed suit. Tickets cost more, and the audience is quieter. Children who once shouted at the elephants now sit very still, watching a woman climb a rope in silence, and clap only at the end.

Old Town Cinema - Weekend Film Festival

Three days of classics. One screen. Real popcorn.

  • Friday 8 p.m. - a silent comedy from 1925, with live piano
  • Saturday 8 p.m. - a Japanese film about a fisherman and his son
  • Sunday 2 p.m. - family afternoon: short clips from fifty years of animation

Tickets for the coming weekend are NT$180 each, or NT$450 for all three shows. Students pay half price on Sunday only.

The Sunday show is the only one suitable for children under ten. Doors open thirty minutes before each show, and latecomers must wait for the first break.

Notice: Auditions for the Spring Play

The drama club will cast its spring play on 3 and 4 April in Studio B.

  • Any student may try out, including those with no stage experience.
  • Prepare one short speech of about two minutes; a list of suitable speeches is on the club website.
  • Bring your own copy of the speech. No music is needed this year.

Important: the play is a tragedy, and three of the eight parts are for older characters. Students who are willing to play these parts should say so at the door.

Results will be posted outside the office on 7 April. Please do not phone the teacher.
