Passage 1
The Corner Everybody Knew

For eleven years the corner of Green Road and Hill Street had an accident about once a month. Everybody who lived near it knew. (11) Letters were written. A mother who lived there counted the cars for three weeks and took her paper to the office. Nothing changed until a shop owner put up his own mirror and paid for it, (12) drivers could finally see what was coming. The town put up a real one four months later. (13), the corner has had no accident in two years. The strange part is not that it was fixed, (14) that everybody knew about it for eleven years first.

全文中譯有十一年的時間，綠路與山街的轉角大約每個月就發生一次車禍。住在附近的每個人都知道。問題是沒有人負責處理。有人寫了陳情信。一位住在那裡的媽媽數了三個星期的車流，把資料送到公所。什麼都沒有改變，直到一位店主自己出錢裝了一面鏡子，駕駛人才終於看得到來車。四個月後鎮上裝了一面正式的。從那以後，這個轉角兩年來沒有再發生車禍。奇怪的不是它最後被解決了，而是在那之前，每個人都已經知道了十一年。

Passage 2
Why We Feel Safe in the Wrong Places

People are afraid of sharks and plane accidents, and not afraid of stairs. (15) Stairs, hot water and small falls at home hurt far more people every year than any animal does. The reason is that fear follows stories, not numbers. A shark is on the news because it is rare, (16) a fall in a bathroom is not news at all. This matters because we spend our care in the wrong place. (17), a family may talk for an hour about a trip to the sea and never once look at the mat by the bath. Safety is seldom exciting, (18) that is exactly why it is easy to forget.

全文中譯人們害怕鯊魚和空難，卻不怕樓梯。然而數字說的是另一回事。樓梯、熱水和家中的小跌倒，每年傷害的人遠多於任何動物。原因是恐懼跟著故事走，而不是跟著數字走。鯊魚會上新聞，是因為牠罕見，而浴室裡跌一跤根本不算新聞。這很重要，因為我們把注意力花錯了地方。例如，一家人可能花一小時討論去海邊的旅行，卻從來沒有看過浴缸旁邊那塊墊子一眼。安全很少令人興奮，而那正是它容易被忘記的原因。

NOTICE
Before the Mountain Walk

Saturday · Leaves 6:30 · Back before dark

  • Two people may never walk alone; stay in threes
  • The last person in each group carries the red bag
  • If anybody feels dizzy or cannot go on, the whole group stops

The path is not hard, but it is long enough to be serious. Most accidents on this mountain happen on the way down, when people have walked all day and go faster than they should.

There is no phone service after the second bridge. This is why the rule about threes matters: one person stays, one goes for help. Everyone must accept this rule; the group master decides, not the user of the path. If you cannot accept it, please do not come on Saturday. Nobody will think worse of you.

全文中譯

星期六 · 6:30 出發 · 天黑前返回

  • 絕不可兩人單獨行動，請三人一組
  • 每組最後一位負責背紅色包包
  • 若有人頭暈或走不動，全組一起停下

步道不難，但長到不能輕忽。這座山大多數的意外發生在下山途中，因為人走了一整天，走得比應該的還快。

過了第二座橋就沒有手機訊號。這就是三人一組規定的意義：一人留下，一人去求援。每個人都必須接受這條規定；由領隊決定，而不是走這條路的人自己決定。如果你無法接受，星期六請不要來。沒有人會因此看輕你。

ARTICLE
The Accident That Was Waiting

After something goes wrong, people look for the one person who made the mistake. Often there is one. Almost always there is more.

A driver runs a red light and may end up in prison, but the light had been slow for a year and nobody thought much about it. A worker falls, but the only way up was broken and there was no other. The person at the end of the line is the one we see first and the cheapest to blame.

Places that become safer are usually the ones that stop asking who and start asking what let this happen. It is a slower question and it makes nobody feel better on the day. But it is the only one that stops the same accident from waiting again next year.

全文中譯

出事之後，人們會去找那個犯錯的人。通常確實有一個。但幾乎總是不只一個。

一位駕駛闖了紅燈，可能因此入獄，但那個號誌已經慢了一年，卻沒有人多想。一名工人摔了下來，但唯一的上去方式是壞的，而且沒有別的。站在最末端的那個人最先被看見，也最便宜歸咎。

真正變得更安全的地方，通常是那些不再問「是誰」，而開始問「是什麼讓這件事發生」的地方。那是一個比較慢的問題，當天不會讓任何人好過。但它是唯一能阻止同樣的意外明年再度等在那裡的問題。
