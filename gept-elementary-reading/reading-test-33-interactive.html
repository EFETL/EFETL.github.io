Passage 1
The Island That Grew

In 1963 an American boat near the coast of one northern country saw smoke coming out of the sea. It was not a ship on fire. (11) Within several weeks there was land where the map showed only water, and within two years the island was a mile across. Nobody was allowed to set foot on it, (12) scientists wanted to watch what would come first. A seed arrived in the mouth of a bird. Then a plant, then an insect, then a low gray plant. (13), a new piece of the world was built in front of people who were writing it down. We usually see land as something that was always here, (14) this one had a birthday.

全文中譯一九六三年，一艘美國船在某個北方國家的海岸附近，看見海面冒出煙來。那不是著火的船。海底有一座山正在裂開噴發。幾個星期之內，地圖上原本只有海水的地方出現了陸地；兩年之內，這座島有一英里寬。沒有人被允許踏上它，因為科學家想觀察最先到來的是什麼。一顆種子隨著鳥的嘴巴抵達。接著是一株植物、一隻昆蟲、一株低矮的灰色植物。就這樣，世界的一塊新土地，在正記錄著它的人們眼前誕生了。我們通常把陸地看成本來就在那裡的東西，但這一塊有生日。

Passage 2
Why Maps Are Never Finished

A map looks like a fact, but every map is a choice. (15) A map made for a sailor shows deep water and rocks; a map made for a walker shows hills and paths, and leaves the sea almost blank. Neither is wrong. Coasts move, rivers change their beds, and a plain that was green in one memory is gray in another, (16) paper cannot follow all of it. (17), every map is a picture of one moment, made for one kind of person. This is worth remembering when we look at a map of somewhere we have never been. It shows us what somebody chose to show, (18) what they left out may be the part we would have cared about.

全文中譯地圖看起來像事實，但每一張地圖都是一種選擇。你先得決定要留下什麼。為水手做的地圖標出水深與礁石；為步行者做的地圖標出山丘與小徑，而把海幾乎留成空白。兩者都沒有錯。海岸會移動，河流會改道，一段記憶中翠綠的平原，在另一段記憶裡是灰色的，而紙張無法跟上這一切。因此，每一張地圖都是某一個瞬間的圖像，為某一種人而畫。當我們看著一張從未去過的地方的地圖時，這件事值得記住。它呈現的是某個人選擇呈現的東西，而他們略去的部分，也許正是我們會在意的那一塊。

A Letter about a Changed Coast

Dear Miss Wu,

You asked us to write about a place that has changed. I chose the beach below my grandmother's village on the west coast.

When my mother was a child, the sand went out about two hundred meters. In an old photo she is standing far from the water. In the golden light of evening today, the sea reaches the road, and the small temple that used to be behind the beach now has water at its back wall twice a month.

My grandmother does not say the word climate. She says the sea has become greedy. I am not sure the two ways of saying it mean different things. I would like to write about this for the class project if that is all right.

Lin Chia

全文中譯

林嘉

ARTICLE
The Land Under the Names

Every hill and river in this country carries a name, and most names were given twice. One name came from the people who lived there first; another was written later on a formal, official map.

Sometimes the two are close. Often they are not. A river called Clear Water in one language may be named after a person in the other. When only one name is printed, a memory quietly disappears.

Some towns now put both names on the sign. It costs almost nothing and changes nothing on the ground. But a child who reads two names learns something a single name can never teach: that this place was here, and full of people, before the map arrived.

全文中譯

這個國家的每一座山、每一條河都有名字，而大多數的名字被取過兩次。一個名字來自最早住在那裡的人；另一個則是後來寫在正式官方地圖上的。

有時候兩者很接近，但常常不是。一條在某種語言裡叫做「清水」的河，在另一種語言裡可能以某個人的名字命名。當只有一個名字被印出來，一段記憶就悄悄消失了。

現在有些鄉鎮把兩個名字都放在路牌上。這幾乎不花錢，地面上也沒有任何改變。但一個讀到兩個名字的孩子，學到了單一名字永遠教不了的事：這個地方在地圖到來之前就已經在這裡，而且充滿了人。
