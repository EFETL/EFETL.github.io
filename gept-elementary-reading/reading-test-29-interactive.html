Passage 1
The Year Our Class Voted Twice

Last year our class had an election for class leader. Two students stood, and one won by three votes. (11) Some students said their names had not been on the paper. Our teacher did not decide the matter herself. She asked us to read the school rule aloud, and the rule said every member of the class must be able to vote. (12) we held the election again on the Friday. The same student won, this time by eleven votes, and we had a great dinner together that night. Nothing about the result changed, (13) something else did. A rule that is followed even when the result stays the same is the only kind anybody will trust, (14) that is what the second vote taught us.

全文中譯去年我們班選班長。有兩位同學參選，其中一位以三票之差勝出。結果出來後，馬上就有問題。有些同學說他們的名字不在名冊上。老師沒有自己下決定。她要我們把校規大聲唸出來，而規則寫著班上每一位成員都必須能夠投票。因此我們在星期五重新投了一次。同一位同學獲勝，這次多了十一票，那天晚上我們一起吃了一頓很棒的晚餐。結果沒有任何改變，但有別的東西改變了。一條就算結果不變也照樣遵守的規則，才是任何人願意信任的規則，而這正是第二次投票教我們的事。

Passage 2
What a Public Service Really Is

A bus that runs at six in the morning with two people on it looks like a waste of money. (15) Those two people may be a nurse and a cleaner, and a large hospital runs on regular buses like this one, and without that bus the hospital does not open on time. A public service is not judged the way a shop is. A shop may close a branch that few people use, (16) a country cannot close the road to a small village and call it good business. (17), some services are built to be there rather than to be busy. We pay for them in years when we do not need them, (18) that is exactly the point.

全文中譯早上六點只載兩個人的公車，看起來像是浪費錢。但這個算法漏掉了一件事。那兩個人可能是一位護士和一位清潔人員，而一間大醫院正是靠這樣的定班車在運作，而沒有那班車，醫院就無法準時開門。公共服務不能用衡量商店的方式來評斷。商店可以關掉少人光顧的分店，但一個國家不能關掉通往小村莊的道路，還說這是好生意。換句話說，有些服務存在的目的是「在那裡」，而不是「很熱鬧」。我們在用不到它的那些年裡付錢，而這正是重點所在。

NOTICE
How to Ask the Town Office for Something

This notice explains how any citizen can ask for a change in our town. It follows the policy of the town government.

  • Write your idea in one page and leave it at the front desk
  • Twenty names from the same town must be on the page
  • The office must answer within one month, in writing

You do not need a lawyer, and there is no fee. If the answer is no, the office must give a reason. You may then ask once more, and a second officer will look at it.

Last year eleven ideas came in. Four were carried out: a light at the corner by the pool, a later bus on Sunday, a bench at the bus stop, a cool room at the God of War temple, and free water at the sports center. Ideas that were not taken up are kept on the wall by the door, with the reason beside each one.

全文中譯

本公告說明任何公民如何為本鎮提出改變的建議。本辦法依鎮公所政策辦理。

  • 把你的想法寫在一頁紙上，交到櫃檯
  • 紙上必須有本鎮二十個人的姓名
  • 公所必須在一個月內以書面回覆

你不需要律師，也不用付費。如果答覆是否定的，公所必須說明理由。你可以再提一次，會由第二位承辦人重新審視。

去年共收到十一件建議，其中四件實現了：泳池轉角的一盞燈、星期天加開的一班公車、公車站的一張長椅、關帝廟的一間涼爽休息室，以及運動中心的免費飲水。未被採納的建議會貼在門邊牆上，旁邊寫著理由。

A Letter about a Bus Stop

Dear Sir,

I am writing about the bus stop outside the old barber shop on Center Road. The bench was taken away in March and has not come back.

I know a bench is a small thing. But four people over seventy wait there every morning, and two of them now stand for twenty minutes. One has stopped taking the bus and pays for a taxi twice a week, which is really too much for her.

I have 21 names on the page I left at your desk last Thursday. I am writing to ask whether it arrived, and when I may expect an answer. Your notice says one month; that month ends on the 11th.

Sincerely, Kao Mei

全文中譯

我想反映中央路上舊理髮店外那個公車站的事。長椅在三月被移走，至今沒有裝回。

敬上，高美

ARTICLE
Rules We Cannot See

When people talk about law, they think of a court and a judge. Yet most rules that hold a country together are never used in a court at all.

We stand in line. We leave the seat by the door for somebody who may need it. We speak more quietly in a hospital than in a market. Nobody made us learn these, and no officer writes them down.

These quiet rules are strong, but they are easy to break and slow to build. A modern government can pass a law in a month; it cannot pass a habit. Even a president cannot. This is why a place where people wait their turn is worth more than it looks, and why a small act of respect is never only small.

全文中譯

人們談到法律時，想到的是法庭和法官。然而大多數把一個國家維繫在一起的規則，從來不會進到法庭。

我們排隊。我們把靠門的位子留給可能需要的人。我們在醫院裡說話比在市場小聲。沒有人逼我們學這些，也沒有公務員把它們寫下來。

這些安靜的規則很強，卻容易被打破、難以建立。一個現代政府可以在一個月內通過一條法律，卻無法通過一個習慣。連總統也做不到。這就是為什麼一個人們會排隊等待的地方，價值比看起來更高，也是為什麼一個微小的尊重舉動，從來都不只是微小。
