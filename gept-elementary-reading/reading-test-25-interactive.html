Passage 1
The Guy in the Next Bed

I stayed nine nights in a hospital in Kaohsiung when I was fifteen. The guy in the next bed was sixty, had cancer, and had been there for four months. He never once asked what was wrong with me. (11) He would tell me which nurse worked at night and when the light in the yard came on against the evening sky. I was afraid of the shots, (12) he always looked at the ceiling at that moment and started a long story about his brother. He left before I did. (13) I understand that he was doing the only thing he could. A hospital takes away almost everything a person can give, (14) attention is still free.

全文中譯我十五歲時在高雄一家醫院住了九個晚上。隔壁床的男人六十歲，得了癌症，已經住了四個月。他從來沒有問過我生了什麼病。他問的是別的事。他會告訴我哪位護士值夜班，還有院子裡的燈什麼時候會在暮色的天空下亮起。我很怕打針，但每到那個時刻，他總是看著天花板，開始講一個關於他哥哥的長故事。他比我先出院。現在我明白了，他做的是他唯一能做的事。醫院幾乎奪走一個人能給出的所有東西，但注意力仍然是免費的。

Passage 2
Why We Wait So Long

Most people in this country see a doctor within a day of feeling ill. That sounds good, and for a broken arm it is. (15) A doctor who has six minutes for each patient cannot ask what else is going on in a life. A cough may be a cough, (16) it may also be a room with no air and a job that never stops. Some hospitals in the region now give each new patient twenty minutes with a nurse first. The nurse asks about sleep, food, and home. (17), the doctor gets a fuller picture before the door even opens. The waiting room is not shorter, (18) fewer people come back three times with the same problem.

全文中譯這個國家多數人在感覺不舒服後一天之內就能看到醫生。這聽起來不錯，對斷掉的手臂來說也的確如此。但速度是有代價的。一位每個病人只有六分鐘的醫生，沒辦法問清楚這個人生活裡還發生了什麼事。咳嗽可能就只是咳嗽，但它也可能是一個不通風的房間，加上一份永遠停不下來的工作。這個地區有些醫院現在讓每位新病人先和護士談二十分鐘。護士會問睡眠、飲食和家裡的狀況。這樣一來，醫生在門還沒打開前就得到更完整的圖像。候診室並沒有變短，但因為同樣問題回診三次的人變少了。

A Letter to a Nurse

Dear Nurse Lin,

You will not remember me. I was the boy with the cut hand in Room 12 last August, the one who would not let anybody near him.

ARTICLE
The Question Doctors Are Taught to Ask

For a long time, a doctor was trained to ask one question: what is wrong with you? It is a good question, and it finds a broken bone quickly.

Some schools now teach a second one: what has happened to you? The first question looks for a part of the body. The second looks for a life. A sick child who cannot sleep may not need a drug; he may need a quiet house. Every human being carries more than one story.

This change is slow, because the second question takes longer and cannot be measured well. But doctors who ask it say something simple: people get better faster when somebody has heard the whole story, not only the part that shows on the skin.

全文中譯

長久以來，醫生被訓練問一個問題：你哪裡不舒服？這是個好問題，而且能很快找出骨折。

現在有些學校開始教第二個問題：你身上發生了什麼事？第一個問題找的是身體的某個部位，第二個問題找的是一段人生。一個生病、睡不著的孩子也許不需要藥，他需要的是一個安靜的家。每一個人身上都不只一個故事。

這個改變很慢，因為第二個問題花的時間更長，也很難被衡量。但會問這個問題的醫生說了一件很簡單的事：當有人聽完了整個故事，而不只是皮膚上看得見的那部分，人會好得更快。
