Passage 1
The Bridge That Changed a Town

Before the bridge was built, people in our town had to drive twenty kilometers north to cross the river. A trip to the hospital could take an hour. (11) The new bridge opened on a rainy day in May, and the head of the town said it would bring the two sides together. He was right about the cars, (12) he was wrong about something else. Young people now find it easy to leave, and many do. (13), the town has fewer families than it had ten years ago. A bridge carries people in both directions, (14) nobody thought about that at the opening.

全文中譯在橋蓋好之前，我們鎮上的人必須往北開二十公里才能過河。去一趟醫院可能要花一小時。對一位病人來說，那一小時可能攸關性命。新橋在五月一個下雨天通車，鎮長說它會把兩岸連在一起。他對車流的判斷是對的，但有另一件事他看錯了。年輕人現在覺得離開很容易，而許多人真的離開了。結果，鎮上的家庭比十年前更少。橋是雙向承載人的，但通車那天沒有人想到這一點。

Passage 2
Two Wheels in the City

Ten years ago, riding a bicycle to work in this city was only for the brave. Cars used every meter of the road, and there was no safe way to cross the big crossing near the station. (15) The city painted a green line along six main roads, put a parking lot for bicycles beside the railroad station, and kept one tunnel for bicycles only. The number of people who ride has grown every year (16) then. Some drivers are not happy. They say the green line takes space away from cars. (17), the city says the same space now carries more people, not fewer. The change did not happen because people suddenly loved bicycles, (18) because the city made the safe choice the easy one.

全文中譯十年前，在這個城市騎腳踏車上班只適合勇敢的人。汽車用掉了路上的每一公尺，車站附近那個大路口根本沒有安全的穿越方式。後來市政府決定試試新的做法。市府沿著六條主要道路畫上綠線，在火車站旁設了腳踏車停車場，並把一條隧道留給腳踏車專用。從那之後，騎車的人每年都在增加。有些駕駛人並不高興。他們說綠線把空間從汽車那裡拿走了。然而，市府表示同樣的空間現在承載了更多人，而不是更少。這個改變不是因為大家突然愛上腳踏車，而是因為市府讓安全的選擇變成最容易的選擇。

A Note to the Bus Company

Dear Sir,

I ride Bus 12 to work every day, and I want to tell you about your driver on the 7:40 bus, Mr. Chang.

ARTICLE
The Road Is Never Only a Road

A road looks like a simple thing: a flat line that takes you from one place to another. The longest one in Russia runs for thousands of kilometers, and its story is the story of a country. But every road is also a choice about who matters.

When a city makes a road wider, cars move faster, and the people who live along it cross less often. Children stop playing outside and stay in the living room. An old woman may keep herself at home for days. The road has not become worse for driving; it has become worse for living.

Some cities now work the other way. They make one road narrow again, plant a line of flowers, and let buses and bicycles use the middle. Cars still pass, only more slowly. The truth is that a street belongs to everyone who stands on it, not only to the person behind the wheel.

全文中譯

道路看起來很單純：一條把你從一個地方帶到另一個地方的平直線。俄羅斯最長的一條綿延數千公里，它的故事就是一個國家的故事。但每一條路也是一個關於「誰重要」的選擇。

當城市把路拓寬，車子開得更快，住在路旁的人就更少穿越。孩子不再到外面玩，只待在客廳。老太太可能好幾天把自己關在家裡。這條路對開車來說沒有變差，卻對生活變差了。

有些城市現在反其道而行。他們把一條路再度變窄，種上一排花，讓公車和腳踏車使用中間。車子仍然通行，只是慢一些。事實是，街道屬於每一個站在上面的人，而不只是方向盤後面的那一位。
