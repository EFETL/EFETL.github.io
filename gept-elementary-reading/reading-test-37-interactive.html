Passage 1
The Wall Nobody Finished

In the north of our county there is a stone wall that runs for two miles and then simply stops. It was started in the 15th century by a king who feared a military force from the sea. (11) Some say the money ran out; others say the enemy never came. The truth is simpler: the king died, and the next one feared other things. Farmers took the stones for their own walls, (12) today you can find a piece of a castle in a pig house. (13), the wall is the most visited place in the county. The view is beautiful and the path is full of visitors, but people do not come to see what was built, (14) to stand where somebody changed his mind.

全文中譯在我們縣的北部，有一道石牆綿延兩英里，然後就這樣停住了。它在十五世紀由一位害怕海上來的軍事力量的國王開始興建。沒有人確切知道為什麼它從未完成。有人說是錢用完了，也有人說敵人根本沒來。真相比較單純：國王死了，下一位國王害怕的是別的事。農夫把石頭搬去砌自己的牆，所以今天你可以在豬舍裡找到城堡的一塊石頭。如今，這道牆是全縣最多人造訪的地方。那裡風景很美，步道上滿是遊客，但人們來不是為了看蓋好的東西，而是為了站在某個人改變心意的地方。

Passage 2
Why Old Stories Change

Every country tells stories about its own beginning, and every one of those stories has been changed. (15) A story is repeated because it is useful now, not because it is correct. A king who lost a war may become a king who chose peace. A war that began over land may be remembered as a war about a word. None of this is a lie, (16) each teller believed what he said. (17), history is not a box of facts but a conversation that never ends. The useful question is not only what happened, (18) who is telling me, and what do they need me to feel?

全文中譯每個國家都會講關於自身起源的故事，而每一個這樣的故事都被改動過。這種改動通常不是一次完成的。一個故事之所以被一再重複，是因為它現在有用，而不是因為它正確。一位打了敗仗的國王，可能變成一位選擇和平的國王。一場為土地而起的戰爭，可能被記成一場為了某個詞的戰爭。這一切都不算說謊，因為每一位說故事的人都相信自己所說的。換句話說，歷史不是一個裝滿事實的盒子，而是一場永不結束的對話。有用的問題不只是「發生了什麼」，而是「是誰在告訴我，他們需要我有什麼感覺」。

School History Project

Grade 8 · Hand in at the beginning of March

Choose one building, road or bridge in this town and find out what was there before it. The principal will read the best three.

  • Talk to at least one person over seventy; a mechanic, a farmer, anyone is OK
  • Find one paper, photo or map from before 1990

You may not use only what you find online. At least one of your sources must be a person or a paper you have held in your hand. If the person you talk to cannot remember clearly, write that down too; it is part of the answer.

ARTICLE
Who Gets a Statue

Walk through any old town and count the stone figures. Most of them show men who took armies to war. Almost none are of the people who built the roads under your feet.

This is not because nobody thought those people mattered. It is because a stone figure costs money, and for most of history the people with money were the people with power. A town remembers what somebody had the money to have remembered.

Some towns now put up small signs: the name of the woman who ran the first school, the family who kept the river boat for eighty years. They cost almost nothing. But a child who reads one learns that history is not only made by the people on horses.

全文中譯

走過任何一座老城鎮，數一數那裡的石像。多數是率領軍隊的男人，幾乎沒有一座是那些鋪了你腳下道路的人。

這不是因為沒有人認為那些人重要，而是因為石像要花錢，而在歷史上大多數時候，有錢的人就是有權的人。一個城鎮記得的，是某個人出得起錢讓它被記得的東西。

現在有些城鎮改為豎立小小的牌子：辦了第一所學校的那位女士的名字、經營渡船八十年的那個家族。它們幾乎不花錢。但讀到其中一塊的孩子會學到：歷史並不只是由騎在馬上的人創造的。
