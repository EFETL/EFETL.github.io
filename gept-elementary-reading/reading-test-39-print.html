Passage 2
Why a City Needs Cheap Places

Every famous part of a city was once a cheap one. (15) A young cook, a new band, a small group with an idea and no money: none of them can start where the rent is high. They need a room that is not expensive and a street that does not yet care what they do. A colorful, fashionable sign costs nothing to hang. A city that loses all its cheap places keeps its buildings, (16) it stops making anything new. (17), the shops people travel to see are the result of years when nobody was watching. You cannot plan that part, (18) you can leave room for it.

NOTICE
Free Winter Meals at the Town Hall

Every Tuesday and Friday · 17:30 to 19:00 · December to February

A hot meal is served to anybody who comes, rich or poor; this is a social meal, not a test. You do not need to show a paper or give a name, and nobody will ask you any question.

  • Children under twelve must come with an adult
  • Food may be taken home in your own box
  • If you would like to help us, come at 16:30

This is run by local people, not by the city. We are given food by three shops on Green Road, and an assistant from a national company brings one product box each week. Nothing is thrown away: what is left at 19:00 goes to the school breakfast box on Wednesday.

We are not a rich organization. What we have is a kitchen, a list of people who help, and two hours twice a week.

EMAIL
A Letter about a Bench

Dear Mr. Kao,

I am writing about the bench outside the town hall, the one facing the bus stop.

Last month a new bench was put there with a bar across the middle. I understand why: somebody had been sleeping on the old one. But the bar also means that an old woman with a bad back cannot lie down for the ten minutes she needs, and a mother cannot put a sleeping child beside her.

The problem it was built to solve was one person. The problem it has made belongs to everybody who waits there. Could the bar be taken off, and could we talk about the first problem another way?

Chen Mei

ARTICLE
The People a City Counts

A city knows how many cars pass a corner each hour. It often does not know how many people sleep badly because of the noise.

What gets counted gets money. A road can show a number that rises; a quiet street where neighbors know each other cannot. So the road is built and the street is not protected, and nobody made a bad choice on purpose.

Some cities now count other things: how many children can walk to school alone, how many old people leave the house each day. These numbers are harder to collect and easy to laugh at. A society is bored by slow numbers and pulled toward fast ones, but a city becomes what it measures, and for a hundred years we have measured mostly traffic.
