Passage 1
The Jar on the Shelf

When I was eleven, my mother gave me an empty box and told me to put one coin in it every Friday. She did not tell me what it was for. (11) By the end of that year the box was heavy, and I had stopped thinking about it. Then my bicycle wheel broke. My mother was gentle but did not give me the money, (12) she pointed at the shelf. I paid for the repair myself, and I remember the weight of the box in my hands. (13) I understood the lesson she had not put into words. Money that arrives slowly feels different in the hand, (14) nobody can teach that with a speech.

全文中譯我十一歲時，媽媽給了我一個空罐子，要我每個星期五放一枚硬幣進去。她沒有告訴我那是要做什麼的。一開始我覺得這個規定很無聊。到那年年底，罐子變重了，而我早已不再想起它。後來我的腳踏車輪子壞了。媽媽語氣溫和，但沒有給我錢，而是指了指架子。我自己付了修理費，我還記得罐子在手裡的重量。那時我才明白她沒有說出口的那一課。慢慢累積而來的錢，拿在手上的感覺不一樣，而這件事沒有人能用一場演講教會你。

Passage 2
Why Small Money Is Hard to See

A person who would never pay two thousand dollars for a jacket may pay sixty dollars for a drink every working day. (15) Sixty times two hundred and fifty days is fifteen thousand, more than seven jackets. We judge a price against the thing beside it, not against a year. Banks know this, (16) a small monthly fee is simpler to sell than a large one paid once. Some people now write down every cost for one month, not to feel bad, but to see the shape of it. (17), they often find that the big choices were never the problem. The money did not go in one loud moment, (18) in five hundred quiet ones.

全文中譯一個絕不會花兩千元買外套的人，可能每個上班日都花六十元買一杯飲料。這兩件事看起來完全不同，其實不然。六十乘以兩百五十天是一萬五千元，超過七件外套。我們是拿價格和旁邊的東西比較，而不是和一整年比較。銀行深知這一點，因為每月一小筆費用比一次收一大筆更容易被接受。現在有些人會把一個月的每一筆支出都寫下來，不是為了自責，而是為了看清它的形狀。結果他們常常發現，重大的決定從來不是問題所在。錢不是在一個響亮的瞬間花掉的，而是在五百個安靜的瞬間裡。
