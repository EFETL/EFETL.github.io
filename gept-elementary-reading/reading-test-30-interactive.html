Passage 1
My First Week at Work

In my first week at the office I was afraid of the phone. Every time it rang I hoped somebody else would pick it up. On the third day my boss saw me look at it and walk away. (11) She picked it up herself, said a few words, and put it down in under a minute. Then she said: that is all it ever is. I still did not like the phone, (12) I began to answer it. By the end of the month I picked it up first. (13) I understood what she had done. She showed me how small the thing really was, (14) a thing you have seen somebody do is no longer a wall.

全文中譯上班第一週，我很怕接電話。每次鈴響，我都希望有別人去接。第三天，老闆看見我看了電話一眼然後走開。她什麼也沒說。她自己接了起來，講了幾句話，不到一分鐘就掛了。然後她說：事情從頭到尾就只是這樣。我還是不喜歡電話，但我開始去接了。到了月底，我成了第一個接電話的人。那時我才明白她做了什麼。她讓我看見那件事其實有多小，而一件你親眼看過別人做的事，就不再是一堵牆了。

Passage 2
What a Job Title Does Not Say

Two people can hold the same job and do work that has almost nothing in common. (15) One clerk spends the day on documents; another in the same division spends it calming people who are angry. The title on the door says clerk for both. This matters when a young person chooses a career, (16) a title is often all he has to go on. (17), the better question is not what is the job but what is the day. Asking somebody what he actually did last Tuesday tells you more than any chart, (18) few of us think to ask it.

全文中譯兩個人可以擔任同一個職位，做的事卻幾乎沒有共同點。這件事在大公司裡尤其明顯。一位辦事員整天處理文件；同一部門的另一位辦事員，整天在安撫生氣的人。門上的頭銜對兩個人都寫著「辦事員」。這在年輕人選擇職涯時很重要，因為頭銜往往是他唯一能參考的東西。換句話說，更好的問題不是「這是什麼工作」，而是「一天是怎麼過的」。問一個人他上星期二實際做了什麼，比任何組織圖都能告訴你更多，而我們很少有人想到要問。

Messages and a Job Notice

Wei: My cousin sent me this. Do you think I should try?

SUN TRADE · Office Help, Two Days a Week
Monday and Wednesday, 13:00 to 17:00
Work: papers, phone, and simple charts
Pay 200 an hour

Ann: Eight hours a week at 200 is 1,600. That is not bad for two afternoons.

星期一與星期三 13:00 至 17:00

鄰近捷運綠線陽光站

  • Students under sixteen may not work after 20:00
  • Eight hours a week is the top limit during term

EMAIL
A Note to a Former Boss

Dear Mrs. Yeh,

I worked in your office for two summers while I was at school. I am writing now because I have just been hired at a company in Taichung, and I thought of you on the first day.

You once asked me to do the same simple work for a whole week: put every paper in order by date. I thought at the time that you did not trust me with anything better. ARTICLE
The Work Nobody Puts on a Chart

Look at any office chart and you will see boxes and lines. What you will not see is the person who remembers that the electric printer needs a hard push, or who owns the one fancy pen that never fails, or who notices when a quiet worker has stopped eating lunch with the others.

This kind of work has no name and takes real time. It is seldom done by the director. It is often done by somebody near the bottom of the chart, and it is what keeps the boxes above from breaking down.

When such a person leaves, the company is surprised by how much stops working. Nothing was written down, so nothing can be handed over. The lesson is simple: the map of an office is never the office.

全文中譯

看看任何一張組織圖，你會看到一個個方格和線條。你看不到的是那個記得那台電動印表機要用力推一下、或擁有那支從不失靈的高級筆的人，或是注意到某位安靜的同事不再和大家一起吃午餐的人。

這種工作沒有名稱，卻真的花時間。它很少由主管來做，通常是由組織圖底層附近的某個人做的，而正是它讓上面那些方格不致崩解。

當這樣的人離開，公司會驚訝於有多少事情停擺。因為沒有任何東西被寫下來，所以也沒有東西可以交接。道理很簡單：辦公室的地圖，從來都不是辦公室本身。
